# Who can stop an NQ research decision?

D. E. Shaw describes risk as a responsibility shared by portfolio managers, guided by its Risk Committee. Citadel describes a risk group independent of investment teams and reporting to its CEO. These are different answers to the same operational question. For a small NQ project, the researcher can own the loss estimate while a separately tested account gate can reject the position. A person with authority to reject the research is still needed to claim independent human review. [D. E. Shaw](https://www.deshaw.com/what-we-do/risk-management) · [Citadel](https://www.citadel.com/what-we-do/)

The banking disclosures concern enterprise risk, valuation, capital and oversight. Their validation concepts can inform an individual research desk without importing bank capital rules. The Federal Reserve’s April 2026 SR 26-2 supersedes SR 11-7 and emphasizes risk-proportionate review and effective challenge. A reviewer needs relevant knowledge, access and the authority to contest use. Two scripts sharing the same faulty price feed offer limited independence. ## Applying the controls to a failed-auction forecast

1. **Define the target.** A possible study asks whether a prespecified failed high predicts a lower executable price five minutes later. Define the bid/ask side, latency, treatment of missing quotes and target horizon before the run. Use the same candidate minutes for the price-only baseline and the added-flow model. A mark-to-mark result measures price response. A trade result also needs executable costs.
2. **Audit the data clock.** Pin the dated contract, timezone, vendor schema, file hash and revision. Store event time, availability time and decision time. Freeze prior value-area levels before the tested minute. With OHLCV alone the aggressor-flow question is unmeasurable. Reject cross-roll joins and future profile updates. CFTC Tuesday positions enter any historical forecasting dataset only after actual public release.
3. **Evaluate forward.** Train or recalibrate on earlier sessions, leave overlapping label horizons across the boundary out of training, then score the next chronological block. Record all probabilities, skips and outcomes. Compare paired Brier scores with a prior-only baseline, bin counts and observed frequencies. Estimate uncertainty by resampling whole sessions or blocks. Forecast discrimination, calibration and net trade expectancy answer different questions. [Gneiting, Balabdaoui and Raftery](https://academic.oup.com/jrsssb/article/69/2/243/7109375)
4. **Challenge the decision.** The skeptic tests delayed entries, stressed slippage, roll periods and announcement days. The judge sees discarded variants and the original rejection criteria. A reused holdout becomes development data. Record that fact and reserve future sessions for the next genuine test.
5. **Apply account limits independently.** Use the existing account survival calculation, including open and pending downside, a maximum of two trades per day and integer contract size. The trader’s $75–$100 preference is a ceiling constrained by verified capacity. A missing or stale account snapshot cannot authorize a position. [Account gate](../../docs/risk-input.md)
6. **Review deterioration.** At each scheduled review, compare recent forecast error and realized execution cost with the frozen reference, showing observation counts. A reproducibility failure or breached hard limit warrants suspension. A noisy short-run loss triggers investigation. Log the cause, changed assumption, owner, validation result and reinstatement decision. Replay old decisions using the version that actually generated them.
